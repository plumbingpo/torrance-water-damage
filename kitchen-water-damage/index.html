Kitchen Water Damage: What Torrance Homeowners Should Know

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. We help Torrance homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

What It Looks Like

  • Vinyl floor that has discolored or gone soft by the cabinets or the refrigerator
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. The appliance hides all of it until the floor changes.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Refrigerator Water Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
