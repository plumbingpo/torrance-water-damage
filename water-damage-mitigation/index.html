The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. Repair is a different job. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. In Torrance, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. Each is its own phase of the job. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.
  • A monthly look around toilets and inside sink cabinets catches drips, staining and spongy flooring.
  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.

Signs You Need Water Damage Mitigation in Torrance

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • The leak is fixed, yet cabinets, walls or flooring remain wet.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • An opening in the roof or at a window lets rainwater enter the building.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • A plumber fixed the pipe but nobody has dealt with the water.

Our Water Damage Mitigation Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.
