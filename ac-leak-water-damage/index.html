Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Torrance

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Drywall Below the Air Handler

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Floors Outside the Closet

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.
